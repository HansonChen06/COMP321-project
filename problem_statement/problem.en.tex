\problemname{The Unreliable Guide}

You want to travel through a park from clearing $1$ to clearing $n$. The park
contains $n$ clearings joined by $m$ undirected trails. Every trail has a
positive length, and the trail network is connected. Parallel trails and
trails that begin and end at the same clearing are allowed.

A guide gives you one claimed shortest distance from each clearing to clearing
$n$. Unfortunately, exactly $\lceil n/100\rceil$ of these claims are wrong.
Determine the true shortest distance from clearing $1$ to clearing $n$, then
identify every incorrect claim and give its correct distance.

\section*{Input}

The first line contains two integers $n$ and $m$
($2 \le n \le 200\,000$, $n-1 \le m \le 200\,000$), the numbers of
clearings and trails.

The following $n$ lines contain one integer each. The $i$th integer, $g_i$
($1 \le g_i \le 10^7$), is the guide's claimed shortest distance from
clearing $i$ to clearing $n$. Exactly $\lceil n/100\rceil$ claims are
incorrect.

The following $m$ lines each contain three integers $a$, $b$, and $w$
($1 \le a,b \le n$, $1 \le w \le 10^7$), describing an undirected trail of
length $w$ between clearings $a$ and $b$. The graph is connected. Parallel
trails and self-loops are permitted.

\section*{Output}

First, output the shortest distance from clearing $1$ to clearing $n$.
Then, for every incorrect guide claim, output one line containing the clearing
number and its true shortest distance. Output these lines in increasing order
of clearing number.

All true distances fit in a signed $64$-bit integer.

\section*{Sample Input 1}
\begin{verbatim}

\end{verbatim}

\section*{Sample Output 1}
