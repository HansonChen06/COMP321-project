\problemname{The Unreliable Guide}

\illustration{0.3}{illustration.jpg}{Photo by \href{https://unsplash.com/photos/green-pine-trees-under-blue-sky-during-daytime-az88XUOj5fI}{Eilis Garvey}}

You and your friend Tom are about to head on a backpacking trip in the great outdoors. You plan to hike through a park containing $n$ clearings, interconnected by paths of variable lengths. A shuttle drops you and Tom off at clearing $1$, and you want to reach clearing $n$ on foot, where you plan to spend the night.

To pack enough supplies for the trip, you and Tom must determine the total travel distance. Since you both want to minimize effort, you aim to find the quickest route from clearing $1$ to clearing $n$ (i.e., travel the least possible distance). Luckily, you meet a local guide at the drop-off area who claims to be an expert on the park and knows the shortest distance from any clearing to clearing $n$.

Unfortunately, a falling rock struck the guide on the head during his last trip, causing him to forget the exact distances for $\lceil n / 100 \rceil$ of the clearings. Still wanting to appear confident, he invents a distance whenever he forgets the true value. Specifically, for $n - \lceil n / 100 \rceil$ of the clearings, the guide states the exact shortest distance from that clearing to clearing $n$, whereas for the remaining $\lceil n / 100 \rceil$ clearings, his stated distance is incorrect.

With this in mind, you and Tom need to calculate the smallest possible travel distance from clearing $1$ to clearing $n$ before setting off. Because you and Tom are considerate people, you also decide to help the guide by telling him which clearings he got wrong along with the true shortest distances. You may assume that every clearing is reachable from every other clearing.

\section*{Input}

Input starts with a line containing two integers $n$ and $m$ ($2 \le n \le 200\,000$, $1 \le m \le 200\,000$), representing the number of clearings in the park and the number of paths connecting pairs of clearings, respectively.

The next $n$ lines each contain a single integer $g_i$ ($0 \le g_i \le 10\,000\,000$), denoting the guide's stated shortest path length from clearing $i$ to clearing $n$, given in increasing order of $i$ from $1$ to $n$.

The following $m$ lines each contain three integers $a$, $b$, and $k$ ($1 \le a, b \le n$, $1 \le k \le 10\,000\,000$), representing a path between clearing $a$ and clearing $b$ of length $k$.

\section*{Output}

Output a single integer on the first line denoting the smallest possible travel distance from clearing $1$ to clearing $n$. On each of the next $\lceil n / 100 \rceil$ lines, output two space-separated integers: the clearing number for which the guide gave an incorrect distance, and the true shortest distance from that clearing to clearing $n$. List these clearings in increasing order of their clearing numbers.

\section*{Sample Input 1}
\begin{verbatim}
2 1
7
4
1 2 7
\end{verbatim}

\section*{Sample Output 1}
\begin{verbatim}
7
2 0
\end{verbatim}
